Concert Ticket Fees Explained — What You're Really Paying

A $60 ticket rarely costs $60. We analysed fee structures across 5 major platforms — Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, StubHub, TicketNetwork, and Vivid Seats — to show you exactly what you pay beyond the face price. Based on real ticket observations, August 2026.

Key finding: Service fees add 12–29% to the face price depending on the platform. On a $100 ticket, you could pay anywhere from $112 to $129 total — a $17 difference for the same seat.

What fees do ticket sellers charge?

Every ticket platform charges fees on top of the face price. These fall into three categories:

  • Service fee — the platform's profit margin, charged per ticket. The largest fee by far.
  • Order processing fee — a flat fee per transaction, regardless of the number of tickets.
  • Delivery fee — for physical tickets or express shipping. Mobile delivery is usually free.

The real impact on what you pay

On a $100 face-value ticket, the difference between the lowest-fee and highest-fee platform is $17. On a $200 ticket, that gap becomes $34. Over a year of concert-going, buying from the platform with the lowest fees can save a meaningful amount — which is exactly why we always show total price, never face value alone.

How LiveFair24 handles fees

Every price shown on LiveFair24 is the total price including all mandatory fees. We never show a face-value price and call it the ticket price.

Where a seller does not disclose all fees upfront, we mark the price as "Estimated total" and explain why. We believe you should know the real cost before you click through.
